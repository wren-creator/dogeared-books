**Full description:**

Timmy is nine now, and it has been a year since his dad became his Mom 2. Most things are good. Mom 2 still makes the best grilled cheese on the grill, and she still does the weather report like it's the biggest story of the century.

But lately the kitchen has gone quiet.

Mom 2 is home on weekday mornings, and her coffee goes cold. His moms whisper when he walks into a room. His best friend Max says he isn't allowed to come over anymore, and won't say why. At a birthday party, Timmy hears a grown-up say something about his family that he can't stop thinking about. And somewhere in the middle of all that, he figures out the word for what his mom is, because a news lady says it on TV, and she doesn't sound kind.

Nobody has told Timmy what's going on. So he does what a lot of kids do. He becomes a detective, and he carries the whole thing around like a rock in his stomach.

This is a story about what happens when the grown-ups finally stop whispering.

**What's inside**
- 12 short, illustrated chapters, told in Timmy's own voice
- Full color pictures on every chapter
- Plain, honest language written for ages 8 to 10, easy to read aloud
- A note for grown-ups and a set of questions to talk about together at the back of the book

**What this book is about**
- Kids can tell when something is wrong, even when no one says it out loud, and being told the truth gently usually feels better than being protected from it
- A child is never responsible for fixing a grown-up's problem
- People can believe different things and still treat each other with respect, and faith is personal, something each person gets to work out for themselves
- Some friendships get stretched too thin to hold, and it's okay to be sad about that
- Love doesn't get smaller when things get hard

**A note on how it handles the hard parts**
The book covers a parent losing a job, an unfair job search, a friend pulled away by his parents, and an unkind remark about faith. It does not tell kids what to think about religion or politics, and nobody in it is a villain. It's meant to give families words, and a place to start talking.

*Still My Mom Too* is the second Timmy story, but it stands on its own. You don't need to have read *My Dad is Now My Mom Too* first.
